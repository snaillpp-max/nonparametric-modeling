# Appendix A Numerical implementation of the pulsating sphere

The force-driven sphere has radius $a=0.05$ m, effective mass $M=0.02$ kg, uncoupled natural frequency $f_0=1000$ Hz, and damping ratio $\zeta=0.01$. The stiffness and damping coefficient are $K=M\omega_0^2$ and $B=2\zeta M\omega_0$, respectively, where $\omega_0=2\pi f_0$. The fluid density and sound speed are $\rho=1.204$ kg/m³ and $c=343$ m/s. With reference force $F_{\mathrm{ref}}=0.001$ N, displacement, velocity, and pressure are normalized by $X_{\mathrm{ref}}=F_{\mathrm{ref}}/K$, $V_{\mathrm{ref}}=\omega_0X_{\mathrm{ref}}$, and $P_{\mathrm{ref}}=\rho cV_{\mathrm{ref}}$, respectively.

Reference trajectories are obtained from the coupled structural and radial acoustic finite-element equations. The acoustic coordinate is $\psi=r\phi$, where $\phi$ denotes the velocity potential. The normalized coordinate $y=\psi/(aV_{\mathrm{ref}}\lambda_0)$ uses $\lambda_0=c/f_0$. The domain $0\le(r-a)/\lambda_0\le4$ is discretized with 64 quadratic elements, yielding 129 acoustic nodes and 130 second-order coordinates including the structural displacement. An exact spherical outgoing-wave condition is imposed at the outer boundary. For the prescribed force, the semidiscrete response is evaluated using its matrix exponential representation from zero displacement and velocity. Harmonic excitation is multiplied by the envelope $[1-\mathrm{exp}(-\Omega t/(2\pi R))]^4$, where $R$ controls the onset duration in excitation periods.

Training combines frequency ratios 0.90, 0.96, 1.04, and 1.10 with phases 0 and $\pi/2$ and force-amplitude ratios 0.6 and 1.2. Eight unit-amplitude FEM histories are scaled to obtain the 16 conditions, using the linearity of this example. Each history spans 36 reference periods with 256 sampling intervals per period and $R=0.2$. Mass-weighted POD is applied to the joint displacement and acoustic-potential snapshots without mean subtraction. The 24 retained modes satisfy $U^{\mathrm{T}}M_{\mathrm{FEM}}U=I$, where $M_{\mathrm{FEM}}$ is the assembled mass matrix. Five-point fourth-order differences provide modal velocities and accelerations; sampling every fourth eligible point yields 36,864 training pairs. Two separate conditions at frequency ratios 0.93 and 1.07, amplitude ratio 0.9, $R=0.35$, and phase 0.4 rad provide 4,608 validation pairs.

The network uses 49 inputs, two fixed Tanh hidden layers of widths 128 and 64, and 24 acceleration outputs. Training RMS normalization and random seed 20260912 are used. Readout weights are fitted by SVD least squares with optional ridge regularization, with feature scale and regularization selected using validation trajectories. When required, an offline Schur decomposition and Riccati correction stabilize unstable directions of the learned zero-state Jacobian. The correction is incorporated into the readout weights using the fixed-feature tangent; no FEM operator is inserted into the network. This local correction does not establish global stability. Recursive prediction integrates the learned accelerations with DOP853 using relative tolerance $2\times10^{-8}$, absolute tolerance $10^{-11}$, and maximum step 0.2 in $\tau=\omega_0t$. Pressure is reconstructed from modal velocities and the acoustic-potential basis.

# Appendix B Numerical implementation of the nonlinear piston and duct

The piston is coupled to a closed duct of nondimensional length one, with fluid density and sound speed normalized to one. Piston displacement is written as $X=x/x_s$, where $x_s=\sqrt{10^{-7}}$. The parameters are $K=1800$, $C=1.264911064$, scaled cubic stiffness $\alpha=300$, coupling coefficient $\mu=0.5$, acoustic dissipation $\eta=5\times10^{-4}$, and $\gamma=0.3$. The cubic stiffness in the original displacement coordinate is $3\times10^9$, and the reference angular frequency is $\sqrt{1800}$. Acoustic nonlinearity is zero in this example; the mechanical spring supplies the nonlinearity.

The duct satisfies $p_{tt}+\gamma p_t=(p+\eta p_t)_{zz}$. Defining $H=p+\eta p_t$, the boundary conditions are $H_z(0)=-(x_{tt}+\gamma x_t)$ and $H_z(1)=0$; the reaction on the piston is $-\mu H(0)$. The FEM reference uses 2,048 uniform linear elements with lumped mass. Classical fourth-order Runge–Kutta uses 2,048 steps per period for the five baseline trajectories and 4,096 for the eight amplitude-transition trajectories, with 128 samples recorded per period. The 3,000-period reference uses 4,096 steps per period.

The dataset contains 39 cases. Twenty-six periodic snapshot sets use force amplitude 0.05 and angular frequencies from 43 to 52 in increments of 0.5, retaining both outer responses from 46.5 to 49.5. These snapshots are synthesized using the discrete FEM impedance and a seven-harmonic mechanical representation. Five 64-period trajectories describe startup, two frequency changes, and the two responses at frequency 48. Eight additional 64-period trajectories use force amplitudes 0.04, 0.045, 0.055, and 0.06 at frequency 48, each starting from two response states. The 109,824 original samples are split pointwise into 76,856 training, 16,471 validation, and 16,497 test samples.

The 32 acoustic POD modes are constructed exclusively from training points in the original 31 cases. Pressure fluctuations $p-x_sX$ and scaled rate fluctuations $(p_t-x_s\dot{X})/48$ enter a mass-weighted covariance, with each case normalized by its training energy. The pressure is represented as $p=x_sX+\Phi a$, where the modes have zero mass-weighted mean; its mean is therefore tied to piston displacement rather than an independent state. For augmentation, at most 512 training parents per case receive four random perturbations within ±20%, with each component scale bounded below by 5% of its training RMS. The instantaneous force is unchanged. Acceleration targets for all rows are recomputed by lifting the reduced state, evaluating the FEM right-hand side, and projecting back. The 35,880 augmented samples give 112,736 training rows.

The 67-input, 33-output network has fixed Tanh layers of widths 64 and 32 driven by $X$, together with an affine connection from all state and forcing inputs. The 3,300 readout coefficients are fitted by double-precision SVD least squares with relative cutoff $10^{-13}$. Training RMS normalization and validation-based feature-scale selection are used. Prediction integrates only the learned accelerations with DOP853 at relative tolerance $2\times10^{-9}$ and absolute tolerance $10^{-11}$. The public implementation regenerates initial periodic states on its selected mesh and therefore need not reproduce historical weights or errors bit for bit. Its default prediction spans 64 periods; the 3,000-period horizon is separately selectable.

# Appendix C Numerical implementation of the Westervelt cavity

The domain comprises a neck, $[0,0.25]\times[0.35,0.45]$, joined to a cavity, $[0.25,1]\times[0,0.8]$. Pressure and pressure rate initially vanish. At the neck inlet, the boundary condition is $\partial_n(p+\eta p_t)+p_t=A g(y)r(t/T)\sin(\omega t)$, with $g(y)=\sqrt{2}\cos[\pi(y-0.4)/0.1]$ and $r(s)=[1-\cos(\pi\min(s/30,1))]/2$. The parameters are $\beta=3$, $\eta=5\times10^{-4}$, and reference angular frequency $\omega_0=50.418647180934734$. All other boundaries are rigid. The inlet radiation term is retained in the coupled acceleration equations.

Mass-lumped bilinear quadrilateral elements are assembled on a structured 1024 × 512 background grid. Symmetry about $y=0.4$ leaves 206,081 computational nodes, and twice the half-domain mass defines the full-domain inner product. Classical RK4 uses 1,024 steps per excitation period. Starting at cycle 67, termination requires ten consecutive relative mass-weighted pressure cycle differences below $10^{-3}$, evaluated at 128 matched phase samples, subject to a 1,000-period limit.

The shared POD basis uses 44 cases: amplitudes 0.8, 0.9, 1.0, and 1.1 and eleven frequency ratios from 0.95 to 1.05 in increments of 0.01. Each case contributes 144 pressure snapshots: 16 transient endpoints, 64 samples from the final cycle, and eight phases from each of cycles 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 40, and 50. Mass weighting and case-energy balancing precede snapshot-Gram eigendecomposition. An explicit constant direction is included. Recovered directions are mass-orthogonalized, and the retained rank is fixed at 48.

Network fitting uses 24 cases with the same amplitudes and frequency ratios 0.95, 0.97, 0.99, 1.01, 1.03, and 1.05. Actual FEM trajectories are reintegrated and sampled at 128 phases per period; at most 10,000 uniformly indexed samples are retained across each converged horizon. Disjoint indices supply 1,776 augmentation bases, 1,500 validation states, and 1,500 test states per case. Seventeen levels from −0.2 to 0.2 perturb modal pressure and rate while preserving instantaneous forcing. Nonzero perturbations are labeled by evaluating the nonlinear FEM right-hand side at the reconstructed state; zero-level targets retain the actual FEM acceleration projection. Coordinate-axis anchors add 1,632 rows per case. Selecting 2,400 nonzero perturbations and all anchors gives 96,768 fitting rows. The 20 cases omitted from NN fitting contribute to POD construction and are not independent holdouts of the complete modeling pipeline.

The fixed-hidden double-Tanh network has dimensions 97–4748–4748–48, feature scales 0.003 and 0.03, and second-layer diagonal gain 0.5. Only readout weights and biases are fitted, using double-precision LAPACK gelsd with relative cutoff $10^{-10}$. Normalization is calculated from selected training rows only. Autonomous zero-start prediction advances the learned acceleration with RK4, without online FEM correction or state replacement. Stored field snapshots and offline label arrays use single precision, whereas evolving FEM states, native full-order FEM acceleration evaluation, and NN fitting use double precision. The public example provides data generation, POD and augmentation, training, and integration; parameter-plane scans and the original mesh and rank audit campaigns are outside its scope.
