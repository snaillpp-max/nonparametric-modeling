# Appendix B. Nonlinear piston–duct implementation and reproducibility

The piston is coupled to a linear closed acoustic duct of nondimensional length one, with sound speed and fluid density normalized to one. The mechanical displacement is scaled as X=x/x_s, where x_s=sqrt(10^-7). The verified parameters are K=1800, C=1.264911064, scaled cubic stiffness alpha=300, coupling coefficient mu=0.5, acoustic dissipation eta=0.0005, and gamma=0.3. The cubic stiffness in the original displacement coordinate is 3×10^9. Acoustic beta is zero; nonlinearity arises from the mechanical spring. The reference angular frequency is sqrt(1800).

The acoustic equation is p_tt+gamma p_t=(p+eta p_t)_zz. With H=p+eta p_t, the boundary conditions are H_z(0)=-(x_tt+gamma x_t) and H_z(1)=0. The piston acceleration includes the reaction traction -mu H(0). The historical working discretization uses 2048 uniform linear elements with lumped mass. Fourth-order Runge–Kutta uses 2048 steps per period for the five baseline trajectories and 4096 for the eight amplitude-transition trajectories; 128 samples are recorded per period. The 3000-period historical reference also uses 4096 steps.

The historical dataset contains 39 cases. Twenty-six periodic snapshot sets cover force amplitude 0.05 and frequencies 43:0.5:52, retaining both outer responses at frequencies 46.5 through 49.5. These snapshots are synthesized using the discrete FEM impedance and a seven-harmonic mechanical representation. Five additional 64-period trajectories describe startup, two prescribed frequency changes, and the two responses at frequency 48. Eight further 64-period trajectories use force amplitudes 0.04, 0.045, 0.055, and 0.06 at frequency 48, each initialized from two previously obtained responses. The 109824 original samples are divided pointwise into 76856 training, 16471 validation, and 16497 test samples.

POD32 is constructed exclusively from training points in the original 31 cases. Pressure fluctuations p-x_s X and scaled velocity fluctuations (p_t-x_s V)/48 jointly enter the mass-weighted covariance, with each case normalized by its training energy. The piston state and acoustic mean are retained separately. For augmentation, at most 512 training parents per case generate four state perturbations of ±20%, with component scales bounded below by 5% of the training RMS. Instantaneous force remains unchanged. All acceleration labels are recomputed by lifting the reduced states, evaluating the full FEM right-hand side, and projecting back. The 35880 augmented samples increase training size to 112736.

The 67-input, 33-output model uses fixed 64–32 Tanh hidden layers driven only by X, together with an affine connection from all state and forcing inputs. Its 3300 fitted coefficients are obtained by float64 SVD least squares with relative cutoff 10^-13. Training RMS normalization and validation-based hidden-scale selection are used. Prediction integrates the learned accelerations using DOP853 with relative and absolute tolerances 2×10^-9 and 10^-11.

The portable package regenerates the same case grouping but independently synthesizes initial states on its selected mesh, rather than importing historical settled 1024-element states. It therefore does not claim identical historical weights or errors. Its paper prediction defaults to 64 periods; 3000 periods are selectable. The smoke configuration preserves model dimensions but reduces mesh and trajectory lengths. Dependencies are NumPy, SciPy, and Numba.
